# Introduction.

Imagine being an extraterrestrial on another world, intelligent enough to slice thin sections of rocks and analyze them in a microscope. Now what if a large boulder of fossiliferous material ejected from a terrestrial deposit some past eons ago landed on this world. These extraterrestrials would slice this rock and notice the tiny nummulites in the stone, for now let us assume it was such a nummulitic rock common on Earth. Now the question is: how would they react to such a rock that had landed upon their world, analyzed by its scientists, containing small animals from a remote world unknown to them? Would they classify them as organic species, perhaps similar to long-dead species in rocks they themselves had found on their world of past life? Or would they say: these are special types of crystallization inclusions, natural spectacles formed by the encrusting material and superficially resembling life? How could anyone be sure, assuming no other knowledge of life, what is the correct answer?

The most direct and first answer is to melt (or chemically dissociate in some way) the material and try to reproduce the observed morphological features with the same source material. For example in the terrestrial case, earthlings know that no one could simply take all the atoms of a nummulite fossil, melt it and come up with the original fossil, no matter how complicated the chemical processes invented. Only a biological organism of the species Nummulites nummulites could produce such a feature. Perhaps the artificial creation might resemble it quite well, on close and thorough examination the complexity of the organic; and considering the numerous other inclusions all with the same pattern how could such a complex process be equally followed through natural processes? If they have been formed through processes occurring in the extraordinary low pressures of a proto-planetray nebula, then take the same source material, combine it in the same pressures and conditions and give me the thin sections to photograph. Searching the literature for such thin sections, I have not found any examples (article that claimed to do so but provided no photographic evidence!) and would like to seem some, in fact!
