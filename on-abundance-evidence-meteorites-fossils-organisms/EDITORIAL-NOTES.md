## Verdict

The History is the book's strength: a primary-source lineage from Biot (1803) to Hahn, Weinland, Karsten and Vogt, with candid Assessments. The weak points are:

1. The Introduction promises more certainty, and more chapters, than the text delivers.
2. A few scientific statements that a skeptical reader would use to dismiss the whole book.
3. The evidence sections still lack the controls that separate "looks like" from "is".

## Priority recommendations

1. **Calibrate the claim.** The Assessments conclude "arguable, never demonstrated"; the Introduction reads as settled. A scoping paragraph ("A word on what this book claims") was added; adjust it to your real position, and consider "Evidence suggesting..." in the title.
2. **Counterarguments and Implications** are announced but absent; both are now marked "(in preparation)". Outline below.
3. **Separate bench contamination from earlier contamination.** Optical continuity excludes slide-preparation contamination only. Separate observed falls from finds (hot-desert stones weather and are handled before sectioning).
4. **State, per form, what the polarized-light pair shows:** optical continuity with the host crystal (suggests the form is part of a crystal), or boundaries that follow the form's own walls (the stronger claim). Chirality cannot be read from a thin section; it was removed from Test three and Structure A.
5. **Choose exemplars carefully.** Saratov and Knyahinya are ordinary chondrites; check their petrologic types, because high-type ordinary chondrites were heated on the parent body (the main objection to preserved organisms). "Altered/Unaltered" is not standard meteoritics terminology and the chapters place Murchison (CM2) under "Unaltered": define the terms.
6. **Use morphotype labels, not taxa.** Corals, crinoids and sponges are Earth groups under ~1 billion years old; chondrites formed ~4.56 billion years ago. Say "resembles", and name forms neutrally.
7. **Pre-empt Kirkpatrick.** Your anchor analogy is the nummulite; Kirkpatrick (nummulites in granite and meteorites) is the pareidolia cautionary tale your own Assessment draws. Say so, or change the analogy.
8. **Separate observation from interpretation** in Structures A-G: give a measurement block per structure (size range in micrometers, N forms in M stones, stone and class), then the interpretation, then at least one abiotic alternative (e.g. radial/excentroradial pyroxene chondrule textures, fine-grained rims, CAIs/AOAs, Liesegang banding, spherulites, serpentine/tochilinite fibers).

## Questions for you

- **Q1.** Which claim level do you intend: "fossils are in some meteorites" or "the evidence suggests it"? This sets the title, Introduction and Assessment wording.
- **Q2.** How were the 136 sections obtained (museum, dealer, own preparation), which are falls vs. finds, and what are their classifications and thicknesses?
- **Q3.** In "How I came to look", what are the "particles ... located in positions related to these structures"? Make it a checkable statement.
- **Q4.** For each class of form: optical continuity with the host, or boundaries following the form's walls?
- **Q5.** Define "Altered" and "Unaltered". Why is Murchison (CM2) under Unaltered and Allende (CV3) under Altered?
- **Q6.** When and where did these organisms live: parent-body aqueous period, imported panspermia, or "wreckage of living worlds"? Chondrites are primitive and many were thermally metamorphosed.
- **Q7.** What are the petrologic types of Saratov and Knyahinya?
- **Q8.** Can you provide raw frames and scale bars for stacked/stitched images, so artifacts can be ruled out?
- **Q9.** Will you deposit the photographs in an archival repository (Zenodo/Figshare/OSF)? Flickr `flic.kr/s/...` guest-pass links are not permanent.
- **Q10.** The Introduction says Weinland named "sixteen genera"; the Weinland 1882 section names 15 (3 polycystines, 7 Uranidae, 2 corals, 3 crinoids). Which is right?
- **Q11.** Should Hahn/Weinland taxon names (coral, crinoid, sponge) stay in Structures A-G, or be replaced by neutral morphotype names?
- **Q12.** The "Mickey Mouse" figure: is the name a deliberate pareidolia point? If so, say so in the caption.
